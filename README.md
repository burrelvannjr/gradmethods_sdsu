## 📖 Course Description

This course introduces students to the logic and practice of social science research. Students will learn to critically evaluate published research and design research of their own. Topics include:

- Research ethics
- Theory and its relationship to research
- Variables, measurement, and operationalization
- Causality and research design
- Qualitative methods (fieldwork, interviews, content analysis)
- Quantitative analysis
- Writing up research

Students may also be introduced to computational social science techniques such as automated content analysis, text analysis, and topic modeling.

---

## 🎯 Learning Objectives

By the end of this course, students will be able to:

1. Frame a research question
2. Motivate a research question substantively and theoretically
3. Integrate existing literature and develop new hypotheses
4. Design a study to test those hypotheses
5. Design a data collection strategy with measures that accurately reflect the study design

## 📝 Major Assignments

### Course Preparation Module (50 pts)
Completed during Week 1. Includes verifying email policy, Google Doc submission setup, installing R/RStudio and Zotero, updating your course profile, and posting a video introduction on the discussion board.

### Course Engagement (50 pts)
Active, consistent participation throughout the semester. A lenient attendance policy applies as long as students demonstrate genuine effort to remain engaged.

### Research Question Quiz (10 pts)
One quiz to help students develop a strong, focused research question relevant to their field of interest.

### CITI Certification (10 pts)
Completion of CITI training for SDSU's Institutional Review Board (IRB).

### RQ & Methods Presentations (20 pts)
Two short presentations: one introducing your research question and one presenting three distinct methodological approaches to answering it.

### Draft Proposal Sections (45 pts total — 15 pts each)
Three draft sections submitted progressively: Introduction, Literature Review, and Data & Methods. Designed to build the final proposal incrementally with instructor feedback.

### Final Research Proposal (15 pts)
A 3–5 page research proposal formatted for SDSU's IRB or a funding organization, using ASA/APA citation style.
